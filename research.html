Public research & tools

Work you can inspect.

What does a score actually measure? What evidence makes a result worth trusting? These projects approach those questions through experiments and tools.

01 / Evaluation measurement

Saved-output case study

Math Reward Hacking

When a reward improves, did the answer improve?

A paired arithmetic-training study and a later diagnostic examine how completion limits and answer extraction change apparent performance. The public package includes saved responses, scoring rules, analyses, and an offline verifier.

What the evidence supports: measurement choices materially affect the comparison. It does not establish consistent reward hacking; three training-seed pairs limit generalization, and weights are not distributed.

02 / Research workflow

Local prototype

ReviewOps

What needs review, what evidence is missing, and who made the decision?

A local workspace brings an experiment queue, reviewer assignments, evidence checks, and explicit decisions into one workflow. Compare successive submissions, keep review drafts while switching experiments, and back up the complete workspace for later restore. Synthetic cases show missing or changed evidence.

Evidence scope: startup cases are synthetic. File integrity does not establish scientific correctness; researcher adoption and time savings have not been measured.

03 / Signal processing

Runnable pipeline

Multimodal Response Lab

Can a signal pipeline stay inspectable when its inputs become unreliable?

A local pipeline combines face, gaze, and head-pose proxies with event timing and response records. The offline synthetic walkthrough exercises scoring, quality tracking, export, and reporting without a camera or model download.

Measurement scope: heuristic scores and sensor quality remain separate. The proxies are not validated measures of attention, emotion, or cognition; synthetic output demonstrates software behavior, not human performance.

04 / Research design

Documentation scaffold

Formalization Evaluation Robustness

Does an evaluator's verdict change when a statement's presentation changes but its meaning does not?

A proposed study separates meaning-preserving rewrites from faithfulness to the source statement. The public design includes unchanged-input controls and a plan for inspectable labels, exact inputs, evaluator outputs, and analysis.

Current public status: documentation only. No runnable evaluator, proof certificates, dataset, tests, or experimental results are released in this repository.
